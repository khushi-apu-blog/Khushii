Understanding Failure Beyond Mistakes

The concept of failure was totally different for me but after  working on the Failure Wiki project , it was a very new thing to me because I never had learnt and thought about failure in such a way before . Usually,we always study about success stories but here we looked about failure and why this happened and what went wrong. Our group was group 5 and our case study was The Horizon Scandal. Initially I thought that it was just a technical failure but it is not.  As I explored more , I realized that it was not just a technical failure  but also a failure of management, accountability, and trust.

The most shocking part was how people can easily trust technology over real humans. In the Horizon case, even when there were signs of errors continuously, the software was trusted and considered as a human mistake. When I tried to connect this topic to real life , I realised that we often consider that digital systems are reliable without thinking about their limitations.

From the book The Right Kind of Wrong our group was assigned with chapter 4 The Perfect Storm. After reading the Prologue and Chapter , it also changed my perspective on failure.This idea that not all failures are the same but some can be meant to be learned was a very interesting thing to me. Initially , I found the concepts slightly complicated , especially differentiating between “good” and “bad” failures. But after discussing with my groupmates and after relating this to case study I understood it better. I understood that not all failures are same , but some are necessary.

• Complex failure involves multiple interconnected causes • Intelligent failure helps in learning and cause innovation

In conclusion , I think that this is a very effective way to understand this topic as Information Science students. This case hit us more than we expected as in the future we are going to spend our careers working with data systems, designing interfaces, and making decisions by digital outputs. And also I get to know that failure is not something that should be avoided, but something that should be analyzed and learned from carefully.
